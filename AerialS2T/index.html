In AerialS2T, a UAV receives a language instruction describing a moving target that is initially outside the camera’s field of view. It must locate the target and keep following it by controlling its flight path and camera pitch.

Figure 1. AerialS2T task overview: takeoff, search, acquire, and track under language instructions.

AerialLIFT-10K

AerialLIFT-10K contains 10,127 expert demonstrations, with 106.3 hours of flight and 1.90 million action-labeled steps. Collected in a CARLA-based simulation environment, it covers 6 maps, 12 weather settings, and 93 target models. Each demonstration provides synchronized observations, UAV poses, and joint flight and camera action labels.

Figure 2. AerialLIFT-10K dataset overview across maps, targets, weather, and acquisition statistics.

MoViC

      
MoViC (Motion–View Coupling) extends a base VLA policy with recurrent target forecasting and projective action decoding. It uses RGB observations, a language instruction, and the UAV state to jointly predict flight and camera actions.

      

        
        
Figure 3. MoViC combines recurrent target forecasting and projective action decoding for joint flight and camera control.

      


      

        
Recurrent Target Forecasting retains target information across observations and predicts distributions over target positions at future waypoint times.

        
Projective Action Decoding projects those predictions into the camera views determined by the base policy’s initial actions. The resulting geometry guides residual updates to the flight and camera actions.

        
The policy predicts eight waypoints over a 1.6 s horizon and replans every 0.2 s.

      

    

  


  

    

      
Experiments

      
We evaluate MoViC on search-to-track and tracking-only tasks. On unseen maps, MoViC improves end-to-end success from 10.2% to 16.6% over its π0.5 base policy, and tracking continuity from 26.02% to 31.66%.
